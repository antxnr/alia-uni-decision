Single honours

Single or joint honours?

A single honours degree focuses on one subject for the whole course, for example Psychology BSc. A joint honours degree splits your time between two subjects (see the Joint honours tab).

Neither is more prestigious or better than the other. Universities, employers and postgraduate courses value both equally, and you can get the same degree classification either way. It simply comes down to personal preference: going deep on one subject you love, or combining two that both interest you.

Single honours suits you if…

  • One subject clearly excites you most
  • You want depth and lots of module choice in that subject
  • You want a clear route to a professional body (e.g. BPS, IBMS, GPhC, HCPC)

Joint honours suits you if…

  • You can't choose between two subjects
  • You like seeing how two fields connect
  • You want to keep two career paths open

    Joint honours

    What is a joint honours degree?

    You study two subjects in the time of one degree, usually 3 years (4 with a placement, a year abroad, or in Scotland). The word in the course title shows the split.

    How to read a joint honours course title
    Title looks likeFormatSplit
    Biology and PsychologyJoint honours~50/50
    Psychology with BiologyMajor/minor~75/25

    👍 Pros

    • Keeps two career paths open
    • Stands out if you can explain why the two fit together
    • Same chance of a good degree as single honours
    • Business is easy to add as a minor

    👎 Cons

    • Less depth in each subject
    • Possible timetable clashes between departments
    • Psychology accreditation can be lost on some courses
    • Can feel like double the background reading

    Another option: Natural Sciences (e.g. Durham, Bath, UCL) is one flexible degree mixing biology, chemistry and psychology modules.
